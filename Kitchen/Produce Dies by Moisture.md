# Produce Dies by Moisture

## Idea
Nearly every produce-storage trick is the same principle wearing different clothes: vegetables rot from too much water and wilt from too little, so storage is moisture management.

## Definition
Leafy greens and herbs want very high humidity (postharvest science puts it above 95%) but no pooled liquid, because standing water breeds the slime while dry fridge air pulls water out of the leaves until they collapse. The household solutions all regulate that balance. A paper towel in the container buffers humidity both ways, soaking up excess and releasing it back. Carrots and celery submerged in a jar drink back lost moisture and re-crisp. Scallions stand in water and keep growing. Ginger goes in the freezer, where moisture is locked solid and grating gets easier. Washed-and-dried grapes or lettuce with a towel hold for weeks. Learn the principle and you stop memorising hacks, because every new vegetable is just a question: is this one losing water or drowning in it?

## Source
Moisture-and-humidity principle per the UC Davis Postharvest Technology Center's produce fact sheets; the household methods popularised by Serious Eats and America's Test Kitchen storage trials. Mined from the Green Spice Diaries produce-storage video.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]] running in reverse: in the pan you remove water to build texture, in the fridge you defend water to preserve it, and both reward thinking in moisture states.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Longer-lasting produce stretches the shopping cycle and makes [[Shop Last, Not First]] workable, since an inventory only anchors the week's plan if it is still alive on Thursday; the savings compound with every avoided rescue trip to the store.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It is the perishable wing of [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], the same stock-management thinking applied to the fridge, and it sits beside [[Leftover Makeover]] as the prevention to that note's cure.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Not everything obeys the moisture frame: ethylene-driven ripening (bananas aging their neighbours), light, and temperature-sensitive tropicals like tomatoes degrade for reasons water cannot explain, so moisture is the first lens, not the only one.
