# Mise en Place

## Idea
Separating preparation from cooking removes almost all of the stress from cooking, because the two activities demand incompatible kinds of attention.

## Definition
*Mise en place* — everything in its place — means all chopping, measuring, grinding and opening is finished and laid out before any heat is applied. The reason it works is not tidiness: prep is interruptible, forgiving and slow, while active cooking is time-critical and unforgiving. Interleaving them means you are chopping garlic while onions burn. Separating them means cooking becomes a sequence of simple moves against a pan you can actually watch.

## Source
Codified in French professional kitchens and formalised in Escoffier's brigade system; the cognitive argument for it is made in Dan Charnas' *Work Clean*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It belongs to the general principle of front-loading decisions so execution can be thoughtless, which is the same reasoning behind [[The Sunday Reset]] at the scale of a week rather than a meal. It is also made necessary by techniques with tiny windows, most obviously [[Tempering]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Taken seriously it changes how recipes get read — you read the whole thing once, prep against it, and only then cook, which is what makes [[Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer]] possible at all. Scaled up, the same move becomes batch prep and the [[Two-Cook-Window Strategy]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It is the kitchen instance of a much wider pattern — staging materials before starting work — and it sits beside treating the [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], since both are about doing work now so that the future version of you has fewer decisions.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Full mise en place for a weeknight dinner is genuine overhead, and it generates a sink of small bowls that costs more time than it saves on a three-ingredient dish. It also sacrifices the real efficiency of overlapping prep with long, safe cooking stages — nobody should stand idle for twenty minutes while rice simmers.
