# Shop Last, Not First

## Idea
Plan meals from what you already own and shop only for the filler ingredients that complete them; planning meals first and shopping second is how a third of groceries end up in the bin.

## Definition
The standard order (pick recipes, buy everything, discover you already had half of it) quietly produces the waste the research measures: the average US household discards roughly 30% of the food it acquires, worth about $1,866 a year. Inverting the order fixes the incentive. First scan the fridge, pantry and freezer; let that inventory nominate the week's meals; then write a short list of only the gaps. Fewer filler ingredients means less money spent and less food dying in the crisper, and the occasional no-shopping week becomes a creative exercise rather than a crisis. The lineage runs from Depression-era thrift through the blog-era "pantry challenge" (Jessica Fisher, annually since 2009) to Tamar Adler's cooking-forward *An Everlasting Meal* (2011).

## Source
Waste figures from Yu and Jaenicke, *American Journal of Agricultural Economics* (2020) and USDA's 30-40% food-supply estimate; pantry-first lineage per Fisher and Adler. Mined from the Green Spice Diaries meal-planning and no-grocery-challenge videos, correcting their $125/month figure upward to roughly $155.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It only works on top of [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], since a deliberate pantry is what makes "what I have" a promising starting point, and it turns the scarcity logic of [[Constraints Create Creativity]] into a weekly habit rather than an emergency.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Inventory-first planning is the natural moment for [[AI Remembers What You Forgot]], and it feeds [[The Sunday Reset]] a concrete first step: look before you list. Done consistently it shrinks both the bill and the bin.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Leftover Makeover]], which is the same principle applied to cooked food instead of raw, and beside [[Produce Dies by Moisture]], which extends how long the inventory stays worth shopping from.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Cooking from what you have biases toward what you always have, so exploration needs to be scheduled on purpose or the rotation narrows. And the research cuts both ways: higher-income, healthier-shopping households waste more, because perishable-heavy baskets are exactly the ones that spoil.
