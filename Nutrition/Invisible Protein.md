# Invisible Protein

## Idea
The easiest way to raise a vegetarian's protein intake is not to add protein dishes but to fortify the staples already being eaten, invisibly, so the food stays culturally itself.

## Definition
Unflavoured protein powder disappears into roti dough, dosa and uttapam batter, or morning oats: a tablespoon adds roughly eight grams of protein without changing taste or texture if you start small and adjust liquid. The move matters because the alternative fails socially: you cannot put tofu in every cultural dish, and swapping beloved staples for "protein foods" makes healthy eating feel like exile from your own cuisine. Upgrading the dish instead of replacing it is the same strategy public health used for a century, from iodised salt in the 1920s to the US flour-enrichment order of 1942-43 that wiped out pellagra: put the nutrient inside the staple people already eat, and compliance is automatic.

## Source
Flour-fortification history via the International Food Information Council; home protein fortification of atta, batter and oats is a common dietitian recommendation rather than a clinical guideline. Sparked by the Green Spice Diaries unflavoured protein powder video.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It grows out of the same problem [[Protein Complementarity]] addresses, hitting protein targets inside a lacto-vegetarian pattern, and it borrows the public-health logic of fortifying staples rather than changing diets.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
It is the gentlest possible [[Healthified Indian Classic]], since the classic does not change at all, and it compounds with [[Satiety Over Calories]]: the fortified breakfast holds you longer for the same plate.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Hung Curd Does Mayo's Job]], another swap that upgrades nutrition without announcing itself, and beside the whole-food protein anchors (dal, curd, tofu) that it supplements rather than replaces.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
It is a gap-filler, not a foundation: leaning on powder instead of food inverts the hierarchy, and whole sources still bring fibre, micronutrients and satiety that a scoop does not. Too much powder also wrecks texture, so the dish stops being invisible precisely when you get greedy.
