# Chaas Is an Electrolyte Drink

## Idea
The salted, spiced buttermilk of Indian summers is not just refreshment with a story; it is functionally a mild oral rehydration solution, which is why savoury drinks outlast sweet ones in the heat.

## Definition
Chaas carries sodium, potassium and a little protein in a thin dairy base, roughly the electrolyte profile of a gentle rehydration drink. That composition matters after sweating: hydration research (the 2016 beverage hydration index study) found dairy drinks retained around a quarter more fluid than plain water, because electrolytes and protein slow the water's exit. The tradition got there first: chaas is the household form of takra, recommended in the Charaka Samhita, and desert households in Gujarat and Rajasthan drink it salted after sun exposure. The honest version of the claim is "salty retains better," not "sugar dehydrates": ordinary sweet drinks roughly match water, and sugar only impairs absorption at syrupy concentrations.

## Source
Maughan et al., "A randomized trial to assess the potential of different beverages to affect hydration status," *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* (2016); takra's lineage per the Charaka Samhita. ## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is soured dairy doing double duty, which places it in the family of [[Every Cuisine Has a Souring Agent]], and the roasted cumin and salt ride the liquid the way spice rides fat elsewhere: tradition encoding function.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Reading traditional drinks for their function turns the pantry of "grandmother's remedies" into testable tools, the same move every [[Healthified Indian Classic]] makes; a frozen spiced-buttermilk base also slots straight into [[Flavour Cubes]].

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Hung Curd Does Mayo's Job]] as the other note where a plain dairy preparation beats an engineered product, and beside sports drinks, which sell the same electrolytes with more sugar and better marketing.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
The claim invites overreach: this is a hydration note, not a detox one, and [[The Health Halo Effect]] warns exactly against letting "ancient" and "Ayurvedic" expand the claim past the evidence. Lactose tolerance also gates who the drink actually serves.
