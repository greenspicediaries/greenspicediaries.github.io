# Hung Curd Does Mayo's Job

## Idea
Strained yogurt delivers the thick, creamy, spreadable role of mayonnaise while swapping a fat-only condiment for a protein-dense one.

## Definition
Hanging yogurt in a cloth drains the whey and roughly doubles the protein per hundred grams (plain yogurt sits near 4-5g, strained versions reach 9-15g), while the texture thickens into something that holds garlic, herbs, lemon and spice the way mayo holds nothing but itself. The numbers are stark: mayonnaise runs about 680 kcal per 100g with roughly 1g of protein; thick yogurt runs under 100 kcal with around 10g. The technique is ancient and convergent: labneh in the Levant, chakka and hung curd in India, Greek straggisto commercialised worldwide from the 1980s. Same move, different cloths. A seasoned yogurt layer under roasted vegetables, in a sandwich spread, or as a burger sauce is the same substitution plated three ways.

## Source
Strained-yogurt lineage per the convergent labneh, chakka and Greek traditions; the yogurt-under-vegetables plating popularised in the West by Ottolenghi and Tamimi (*Jerusalem*, 2012). Mined from the Green Spice Diaries sandwich, burger and Brussels sprouts videos, which all reuse the swap.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It belongs to the dairy half of [[Protein Complementarity]], and its mouthfeel works because straining concentrates the gel: the same remove-water logic that drives so much texture work, inverted from [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]] into creaminess.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
As the default creamy element it quietly rewrites sauces, spreads and dressings into a [[Healthified Indian Classic]] pattern, and whipped with cream and honey it even carries dessert, which is what a no-bake yogurt cheesecake is.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Invisible Protein]] as the other stealth upgrade, and beside [[Emulsion]], since mayonnaise is the canonical emulsion and strained yogurt is the shortcut that skips the emulsifying entirely.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Yogurt splits under direct heat where mayo and cream hold, so the swap fails in hot sauces unless stabilised. And [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]] pushes back: fat carries aroma, so the leanest version is also the flattest, and a spoon of good oil back in is often the right correction.
