# Healthy Is Not a Permission Slip

## Idea
Labelling a food healthy changes how much of it people eat, so the label can quietly undo the benefit it was describing.

## Definition
A health framing (low-fat, high-protein, sugar-free, vegan) functions as a licence. People serve themselves more, eat faster, and under-report intake when a food carries a virtuous label, an effect documented as the health halo. The food itself may genuinely be better; the portion response is what erases the gain. Dates, nut butters, protein bars, olive oil, granola and "guilt-free" desserts are the usual sites of this, since all are nutritious and all are energy-dense.

## Source
The health-halo literature in consumer research, notably Brian Wansink's and Pierre Chandon's work on labelling and portion inflation; widely echoed by Indian food creators pointing out that overeating healthy food is still overeating.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It belongs to the broader problem that a single attribute cannot summarise a food, which is also what [[Satiety Over Calories]] is arguing from the other direction, and it inherits from the general unreliability of labels as a decision shortcut.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
For a channel built on healthier versions of craved dishes, this is the integrity line: the honest version of a [[Healthified Indian Classic]] names its portion, not only its swap. Saying so out loud is also the most trust-building move available, which makes it a strong candidate for the authority-building half of the content mix.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It rhymes with every other licensing effect, from the exercise that justifies dessert to the recycling bin that increases consumption, and it sits beside [[The Reframe Is the Hook]] as its uncomfortable twin, since the same reframe that earns attention is the one that grants permission.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Pushed too hard it becomes food moralising, and warning people about portions of lentils and dates is a poor use of anyone's attention when the real problem is usually elsewhere in the diet. For anyone recovering from restriction, permission is the point, and this framing can do genuine harm.
