# Satiety Over Calories

## Idea
How full a meal makes you is driven by protein, fibre, water and volume rather than by its calorie count, which is why some low-calorie meals leave you hungry and some higher-calorie ones do not.

## Definition
Satiety is a physiological response to food volume, protein content, fibre, and the speed at which a meal digests — not a linear function of energy. A 400-calorie plate built on legumes, vegetables and a protein source occupies far more stomach volume and triggers more satiety signalling than a 400-calorie plate of refined carbohydrate and fat. The practical consequence is that designing meals for fullness tends to regulate intake on its own, with no counting involved.

## Source
The satiety index research originating with Susanna Holt's 1995 study; developed in Barbara Rolls' work on energy density and in Stephan Guyenet's *The Hungry Brain*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It belongs to the shift away from calorie arithmetic toward food composition, and it is the mechanism that makes [[Protein Complementarity]] practically useful — a legume-and-grain plate happens to score high on exactly the variables satiety responds to.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Designing for fullness rather than deficit is what makes healthy eating survive a real week, since a meal that leaves you hungry at 4pm has already failed regardless of its macros — which is the quiet precondition for [[The Sunday Reset]] actually holding. It also gives a concrete recipe checklist: protein anchor, fibre volume, water content, and something to chew.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits next to energy density and the glycaemic response as neighbouring lenses on the same question, and it is the nutritional sibling of [[Healthy Is Not a Permission Slip]] — both are corrections to the idea that a single number on a label settles anything.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Satiety is highly individual and poorly predicted by any index, so the generalisations break down per person. It also cannot be stretched into "calories do not matter" — energy balance still governs the outcome, and highly palatable food reliably overrides fullness signals, which is why volume alone is not a strategy.
