# Learn to Cook Indian Food Roadmap

A path from "I follow Indian recipes and they come out flat" to "I can cook from a region I have never cooked from and know what to adjust." Six stages, roughly twelve weeks at two or three cooking sessions a week. Each stage names the atomic notes that carry the concepts, so you can read the idea before you cook it.

---

## Stage 1 — Seasoning, before any recipe (week 1-2)

Nothing else in this path works until seasoning does. Most "flat" Indian food is under-salted, under-soured, or both.

- Salt as a process rather than a final step — [[Salt Early, Salt Often]]
- Acid as a structural requirement and the regional forms it takes — [[Every Cuisine Has a Souring Agent]]
- Learn to taste and correct at every stage, not at the end

**Practice:** cook the same dal three times — once salted only at the end, once salted in layers, once with acid added at the finish. Taste them side by side.

---

## Stage 2 — The spice drawer (week 3-4)

The point of this stage is to stop treating spices as interchangeable powder.

- Why spices need fat at all — [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]]
- The foundational move, under all its regional names — [[Tempering]]
- How grinding changes an ingredient's job and shelf life — [[Whole vs Ground Spices]]
- Which spices build and which spices finish — [[Garam Masala Is a Finishing Spice]]
- Stocking the drawer so it supports many dishes — [[Pantry as Infrastructure]]

**Practice:** one tempering a day for a week, over plain yoghurt or dal. Change one variable each time — the fat, the spice, the order, the heat.

---

## Stage 3 — Heat and texture (week 5-6)

The stage where food stops being soft and grey.

- Browning as aroma manufacture — [[Maillard Reaction]]
- Crispness as a moisture state — [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]]
- Reading the pan instead of the clock — [[Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer]]

**Practice:** roast the same vegetable on a crowded tray and a spacious one. Then pan-fry paneer or tofu dry-surfaced and straight from the packet.

---

## Stage 4 — Structure: dals, sabzis, sauces (week 7-8)

Indian home cooking is a small number of structures with enormous variation inside them. Learn the structures and the recipes stop being things to memorise.

- The legume-and-grain backbone and why it is nutritionally complete — [[Protein Complementarity]]
- Body and richness without cream — [[Emulsion]]
- Savoury depth from plants — [[Umami Without Meat]]
- Building a plate that actually keeps you full — [[Satiety Over Calories]]

**Practice:** one dal, one dry sabzi, one gravy — each cooked twice, changing only the tempering and the souring agent.

---

## Stage 5 — Regions (week 9-10)

This is where the path opens out, because there is no end to it.

- Why there is no single Indian cuisine — [[Indian Food Is Not One Cuisine]]
- How techniques travel while flavour stays local — [[Borrowed Technique, Native Flavour]]

**Practice:** pick three regions you have never cooked from. For each, identify the fat, the staple, the souring agent and the spice baseline before choosing a dish.

---

## Stage 6 — Cooking inside a real week (week 11-12)

Skill that only works on Sundays is a hobby. This stage makes it survive a Tuesday.

- Separating prep from cooking — [[Mise en Place]]
- The weekly ritual that removes decisions — [[The Sunday Reset]]
- Why two cooking sessions beat one or seven — [[Two-Cook-Window Strategy]]
- Cooking components rather than finished dishes — [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]]
- Transforming rather than reheating — [[Leftover Makeover]]
- Setting the cadence by what survives a bad week — [[Consistency Over Intensity]]

**Practice:** run one full week on two cooking windows. Write down what ran out, what went uneaten, and what you got bored of.

---

## After this

The path does not end; it widens. Fermentation, baking and millets are each their own direction, and every region in Stage 5 is a path of its own. The useful next move is not another stage but a constraint — see [[Constraints Create Creativity]] — and the honest warning is to keep the practice modest enough to continue, which is what [[Toxic Productivity]] is there to flag.
