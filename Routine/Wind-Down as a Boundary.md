# Wind-Down as a Boundary

## Idea
An evening routine works less as self-care than as a boundary: a fixed sequence that tells the day it has ended and stops work leaking into the hours after it.

## Definition
A wind-down is an ordered set of actions performed in the same sequence each evening: closing the laptop, moving the body, cooking and eating, then something deliberately unproductive, then sleep. The sequence matters more than the content. Its function is to mark a transition, because without a marker the working day does not end so much as fade, and the hours after it get spent half-working and half-resting while feeling like neither.

## Source
The shutdown-ritual idea in Cal Newport's *Deep Work*, combined with sleep-hygiene research on consistent pre-sleep cues.

---

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It belongs to the same front-loading-and-fixed-cues family as [[The Sunday Reset]], scaled down from a week to a day, and it exists because the boundary between work and rest has to be constructed once work stops happening in a separate place.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
It makes the evening the one reliable slot for cooking, which is what lets a weeknight dinner survive a full day, and it is a content format in its own right, since a wind-down sequence is episodic and recognisable in exactly the way a weekly ritual is. It also protects the thing that [[Consistency Over Intensity]] depends on: enough recovery that tomorrow happens at all.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It is the daily sibling of [[The Sunday Reset]] and the mirror image of a morning routine, and it sits beside [[Toxic Productivity]] as the practice that pushes back against it.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
A routine optimised into a checklist becomes another performance to fail at, and the version filmed for an audience is not the version that actually restores anyone. Rigid sequences also break against real life (travel, late work, small children), and a boundary that cannot bend simply gets abandoned.
