# Cook Once, Eat Twice

## Idea
Deliberately cooking a component in a form that can become two different meals costs almost nothing extra at the stove and halves the number of cooking sessions in a week.

## Definition
The move is to cook the *component* rather than the dish, and to stop one step short of finishing it. A pot of chickpeas becomes a curry tonight and a tikki or a salad bowl on Wednesday. A tray of roasted vegetables becomes a plate now and a filling later. The constraint is that the component must be under-seasoned and under-committed: a fully finished dish can only be reheated, while a neutral component can be redirected. Marginal effort is near zero because the scaling cost of cooking is in the setup, not the quantity.

## Source
Long-standing home-cooking practice; articulated as a principle in Tamar Adler's *An Everlasting Meal* and central to most component-based meal-prep systems.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is the per-dish mechanism that makes the [[Two-Cook-Window Strategy]] work at all, and it depends on the recognition that the expensive part of cooking is the decision and the clean-up rather than the quantity in the pot.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Cooking neutral components rather than finished dishes is exactly what turns storage into opportunity instead of obligation, which is where [[Leftover Makeover]] begins. It also changes how recipes should be written for the channel, since naming the second life of a component is more useful to a viewer than a second recipe.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It is the kitchen version of [[Film Once, Publish Twice]], which is the same leverage applied to content, and it rhymes with [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], since both are ways of having useful things already in the house.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Not everything survives the delay: anything where [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]] is the whole point is worse on day two, and a broken [[Emulsion]] cannot be reheated back together. Under-seasoning for flexibility also means the first meal is slightly compromised in service of the second, and eaten badly it just produces a week of samey food.
