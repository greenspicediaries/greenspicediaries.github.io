# The Sunday Reset

## Idea
A single recurring ritual that resets the kitchen, the fridge and the week's plan does more for how you eat than any individual healthy recipe.

## Definition
The reset is one block of time — groceries, a plan, and a cooking session — placed at the same point every week. Its value is not the food produced but the decisions removed: by Wednesday evening the question is no longer "what should I eat" but "which of these components goes together". Because it recurs on a fixed day it becomes automatic rather than motivational, and because it is visible in the fridge it keeps working even on the days you have no interest in cooking.

## Source
A widely adopted creator and home-organisation ritual; the underlying mechanism is habit formation through stable cues, as described in James Clear's *Atomic Habits*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is [[Mise en Place]] scaled from a meal to a week, and it only works because it is anchored to a fixed cue rather than to motivation — the point [[Consistency Over Intensity]] keeps making.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Run properly it produces the components that the [[Two-Cook-Window Strategy]] draws on, and it is what makes a genuinely quick weeknight dinner possible rather than aspirational. It also turns out to be the strongest content format available on the lifestyle side, because a weekly ritual is inherently episodic and builds a watching habit.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside the evening version of the same idea in [[Wind-Down as a Boundary]] — one resets the week, the other closes the day — and beside the [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], since the reset only has to cover what the shelf cannot.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
It concentrates all the effort into one block, so a weekend that disappears takes the whole week with it, and a reset that becomes a four-hour obligation is a fast route to [[Toxic Productivity]]. Food cooked on Sunday is also genuinely worse by Friday, which is the exact objection the two-window version exists to answer.
