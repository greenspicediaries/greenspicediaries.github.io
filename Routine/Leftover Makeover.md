# Leftover Makeover

## Idea
Leftovers become appealing again when they are transformed rather than reheated, because the thing people are tired of is the *form* of the dish, not its ingredients.

## Definition
Reheating returns a dish to a slightly worse version of what was already eaten. Transformation changes its format instead: yesterday's dal becomes the binder in a tikki, leftover rice becomes a lemon rice or a stuffed paratha, roasted vegetables become a dip or a filling, a stale loaf becomes croutons or a bread roll. The ingredients carry over; the texture, temperature and shape do not. Three moves cover most of it — change the texture, add a fresh acid or herb, and add a new aromatic through a finishing tempering.

## Source
A universal frugal-cooking tradition — Indian leftover-rice cookery, Italian *cucina povera*, bread-based peasant dishes — argued as a principle in Tamar Adler's *An Everlasting Meal*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the payoff of [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]] — only a component that was left deliberately neutral can be redirected — and it depends on having a stocked [[Pantry as Infrastructure]], since transformation needs ingredients that were not part of the original dish.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
The three standard moves make it a technique rather than a set of recipes, and the aromatic one is just a finishing [[Tempering]], which is the cheapest flavour intervention available. As content it is reliably useful in a way recipes are not, because every viewer already has the input ingredient in their fridge.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits inside the [[Two-Cook-Window Strategy]] as the mechanism that stops prepped eating becoming monotonous, and it rhymes with [[Constraints Create Creativity]] — the fridge's contents are a constraint that generates the dish.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Food safety sets a hard limit that no amount of transformation moves: three or four days refrigerated is the window, and rice in particular is unforgiving. Transformation also takes real effort at the exact moment the appeal of leftovers was that they required none.
