# Cultural Specificity Is an Asset

## Idea
Making culturally specific food more generic to reach a wider audience reliably reduces reach, because specificity is what makes a thing worth stopping for.

## Definition
The instinct when addressing a broad audience is to sand off the particulars — call it a lentil stew rather than chainsoo, a flatbread rather than a thepla. In practice the specific version performs better. A named regional dish carries novelty for people outside the tradition and recognition for people inside it, and both are reasons to stop scrolling. "Generic" is not neutral ground; it is the version with nothing in it that anyone was looking for.

## Source
Observable across food creators whose reach scales with cultural specificity rather than against it; consistent with the long-tail and niche-audience arguments in Kevin Kelly's *1,000 True Fans*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the strategic consequence of [[Indian Food Is Not One Cuisine]] — if there are dozens of regional cuisines, then naming the exact one is both more accurate and more interesting — and it belongs to the broader principle that specificity is what makes writing of any kind land.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It means the search-and-discovery surface should be built from exact dish names rather than category names, and it makes the long tail of regional dishes a content supply rather than a risk. It is also the half of [[Borrowed Technique, Native Flavour]] that must not be compromised — the wrapper can travel, the flavour should stay named.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[The Reframe Is the Hook]] as the other half of a working package: specificity supplies the subject, the reframe supplies the reason to watch. It also rhymes with [[Show Your Work]], since both trade broad polish for particular truth.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Specificity does raise the floor of required context — a dish nobody has heard of needs a sentence of explanation that a curry does not — and search volume for an obscure regional name is genuinely low, so discovery has to come from elsewhere. There is also a real line between specificity and inaccessibility.
