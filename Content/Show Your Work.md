# Show Your Work

## Idea
Sharing the process, including the failures and the half-finished attempts, builds a following more reliably than only publishing finished results.

## Definition
Finished work invites admiration; process invites attachment. Showing the recipe that failed three times, the dough that did not rise, the spice that was wrong, the reason a technique was abandoned gives an audience something to follow rather than merely consume. It also compounds: process is abundant and continuous, while finished results are rare and intermittent, so a practice built on process never runs out of material.

## Source
Austin Kleon's *Show Your Work!* (2014), building on the open-notebook and working-in-public tradition.

---

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It belongs to the working-in-public tradition that a digital garden is itself an instance of, and it depends on the recognition that an audience's attachment tracks effort made visible rather than polish.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
It makes the honest version of a recipe, the one that names what went wrong, more valuable than the flawless one, which is also the integrity position behind [[Healthy Is Not a Permission Slip]]. For a food channel it is what converts recipe viewers into people who follow a person rather than a feed.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Cultural Specificity Is an Asset]], since both trade generic polish for particular truth, and beside [[Consistency Over Intensity]], since a process-based practice is one that can actually be sustained.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Process as a performance is its own trap: curated vulnerability is worse than straightforward polish, and failures staged for relatability read instantly as fake. There is also work that genuinely should not be shown while it is forming, and some audiences simply want the finished recipe and nothing else.
