# Fat-Soluble Flavour

## Idea
Most of what we call spice flavour lives in oil-soluble aroma compounds, so a dish cooked without fat cannot access the majority of the flavour its spices contain.

## Definition
The volatile molecules that make cumin smell like cumin and cinnamon smell like cinnamon are largely lipophilic — they dissolve in fat, not in water. Fat therefore does three things at once: it extracts those compounds out of the spice, it distributes them evenly through the dish, and it coats the tongue so the aroma releases slowly instead of all at once. This is why the same quantity of spice tastes thin in a water-based dish and full in a fat-based one.

## Source
Food chemistry of flavour extraction; explained for cooks in Nik Sharma's *The Flavor Equation* and Samin Nosrat's *Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
This is the physical fact that makes [[Tempering]] necessary rather than merely traditional, and it belongs to the larger question of what fat is actually *for* in cooking beyond calories and browning.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It reframes every low-fat recipe as a flavour-extraction problem rather than a willpower problem, which is the honest constraint inside any [[Healthified Indian Classic]]. The practical workaround is to spend a small fat budget where it does the most work — in the tempering, not in the body of the dish — and to lean on [[Umami Without Meat]] and acid for the rest.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside the water-soluble half of the story, where [[Salt Early, Salt Often]] and souring agents do their work in the liquid phase, and it is the same principle that makes an [[Emulsion]] such an efficient flavour carrier.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
It argues against the oil-free cooking that a lot of healthy-eating content promises, and there is no way to reason around it — a no-oil tadka extracts less aroma, full stop. The counter-argument is that some flavour is water- or alcohol-soluble, and that toasting dry spices before grinding recovers part of what skipping the fat costs.
