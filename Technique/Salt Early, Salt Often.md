# Salt Early, Salt Often

## Idea
Salt added in layers throughout cooking seasons food from the inside and needs less total salt than the same dish salted once at the end.

## Definition
Salt has to travel. Added early it dissolves into the cooking liquid and diffuses into the food itself, seasoning the interior and — in vegetables and legumes — changing texture by weakening cell walls and pectin. Added only at the end it sits on the surface, where it reads as sharply salty on first contact while the inside stays bland, so you end up using more salt to chase a seasoning that never arrives. Salting in stages, tasting at each one, gets a better-seasoned dish with less sodium.

## Source
Samin Nosrat's *Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat*, where salt's role as the first of four variables is the central argument.

---

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the water-soluble half of the flavour story that [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]] leaves out, and it belongs to the larger idea that seasoning is a process distributed through cooking rather than a step at the end of it.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Layered salting is quietly one of the strongest tools for healthier cooking, because it lets a dish taste properly seasoned on noticeably less sodium — the opposite of how low-salt cooking is usually framed. It also means tasting has to become a habit at every stage, which is really an argument for [[Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer]].

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside acid as the other great correction — a flat dish often wants lemon rather than more salt — and it interacts directly with the [[Maillard Reaction]], since salt pulls moisture to the surface and can delay the browning you were hoping for.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Early salting genuinely hurts some things: it toughens the skins of dried beans in hard water, draws water out of anything you wanted to keep crisp, and in a reducing sauce it compounds into oversalting with no way back. It also pushes against the medical reality that for some people total sodium, not its distribution, is the number that matters.
