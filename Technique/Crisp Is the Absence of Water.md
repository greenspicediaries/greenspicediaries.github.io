# Crisp Is the Absence of Water

## Idea
Crispness is not a flavour or a cooking time — it is a moisture state, so every technique that produces crisp food is really a technique for removing water faster than it arrives.

## Definition
A surface stays soft while it holds water, because water caps the surface temperature near 100°C and keeps the structure pliable. Crispness appears only when that surface dries out and its starches or proteins set into a rigid, brittle layer. Everything that reliably produces crisp food — high heat, dry surfaces, space in the pan, starch coatings, a second fry, a wire rack instead of a plate — is a variation on getting water out and keeping it out.

## Source
Food-science first principles; applied relentlessly in the deep-fry and roast chapters of Kenji López-Alt's *The Food Lab* and in McGee's *On Food and Cooking*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the precondition for the [[Maillard Reaction]], since nothing browns until it is dry, and it explains why fat matters so much as a heat-transfer medium rather than only as [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]].

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Treating crispness as a moisture problem gives you a checklist that transfers across completely unrelated dishes — pressed tofu, parboiled-and-roughed potatoes, pan-fried rice paper, a tikki that has been chilled before frying all work for the same reason. It also explains the most common failure in healthy cooking: a crowded tray of vegetables that steams itself into softness.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Dough Hydration]], which is the same water-content question pointed at the inside of a food rather than its surface, and beside the staling of bread, where crispness is lost to moisture migrating back outward.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Crisp is also fragile and fleeting — sauce, steam, stacking, or a covered container will undo it within minutes, which makes it a poor fit for the make-ahead logic of [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]]. And the fastest routes to crisp are usually the most oil-intensive, putting it in direct tension with any [[Healthified Indian Classic]].
