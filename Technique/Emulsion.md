# Emulsion

## Idea
An emulsion suspends fat in water as microscopic droplets held apart by an emulsifier, which is how a sauce becomes creamy without any cream in it.

## Definition
Fat and water do not mix; they separate because the fat coalesces back into a single mass. An emulsifier — lecithin in egg yolk or mustard, protein and starch in tahini, nut paste, yoghurt, aquafaba or cooked potato — coats each fat droplet so the droplets cannot merge, holding them dispersed through the water phase. The result reads on the tongue as creamy and thick, because perceived creaminess is mostly droplet size and distribution rather than fat quantity.

## Source
Classical sauce-making (mayonnaise, hollandaise, vinaigrette); the mechanism and the plant-based emulsifier route are covered in McGee's *On Food and Cooking* and Nik Sharma's *The Flavor Equation*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the structural trick that makes dairy-free creaminess possible at all, and it depends on the same fat-and-water division that gives [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]] its power — an emulsion is simply the arrangement that lets both phases carry flavour at once.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Understood as a mechanism rather than a set of recipes, it generates a whole category at once: tahini-yoghurt sauces, no-oil nut-free pesto, cashew cream, hummus, aquafaba dressings, and the butter-bean sauce under a cauliflower steak are all the same move with different emulsifiers. It is therefore one of the highest-leverage techniques behind any [[Healthified Indian Classic]], since it delivers richness on a small fat budget.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It is a ratio-and-technique problem in the same way [[Dough Hydration]] is, and it sits beside starch-thickened and reduction-thickened sauces as a third, quite different route to body.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Emulsions break — too much fat too fast, too much heat, or too little emulsifier and the sauce splits, and a broken sauce needs rebuilding rather than stirring. They are also a poor fit for reheating, which limits how well they travel into [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]], and the creaminess illusion has a ceiling: at some point a dish genuinely does want more fat.
