# Rice Paper's Double Life

## Idea
The same sheet of rice paper shatters like glass when pan-fried and turns gummy when steamed, because it is a pre-gelatinised starch film that simply follows its water content.

## Definition
Bánh tráng is rice and tapioca starch that was already cooked (gelatinised) and dried at the factory. Dip it in water and the starch rehydrates into a soft, sticky gel: that is the chewy summer-roll skin, and steaming only keeps it wetter, which is why steamed rice-paper dumplings stay gummy and weld to each other. Pan-fry it in a little oil and the opposite happens: surface water flashes off, the starch film drops below its glass transition and sets into a brittle, blistered glass that shatters. The crisp is reversible too; trapped filling steam or a wet sauce will quietly un-crisp it from the inside. Double-wrapping is structural, not textural: two layers to hold a filling one layer cannot.

## Source
Traditional Vietnamese bánh tráng; the crispy rice-paper dumpling is a modern invention widely credited to Lisa Kitahara of Okonomi Kitchen (around 2020-21) before going viral on TikTok. Science per standard starch gelatinisation and glass-transition behaviour.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is a textbook case of [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]]: one ingredient, two moisture states, two completely different foods, with no [[Maillard Reaction]] required for the texture (the blister is physics, the browning is bonus).

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Treating wrappers as moisture-state machines explains failures across the category: soggy spring rolls, leathery leftover dumplings, and why a crisped batch must be eaten now rather than boxed, the standing tension with [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]].

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Dough Hydration]], where water content also decides texture but before cooking rather than after, and beside store-bought shortcut wrappers generally, the bridge between scratch cooking and takeout.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
The gummy state is not a failure mode, it is the entire point of fresh summer rolls, so the note is not "fried good, steamed bad" but "choose the state on purpose." And rice paper tears, sticks and overlaps unforgivingly, so the technique has a hands-on learning curve the physics does not mention.
