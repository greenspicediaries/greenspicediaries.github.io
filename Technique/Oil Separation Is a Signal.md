# Oil Separation Is a Signal

## Idea
When oil pools at the edges of a cooking masala, the base is announcing that its water is gone, its raw flavours are driven off, and it is ready: a doneness indicator no timer can replace.

## Definition
A raw onion-tomato masala is an oil-in-water emulsion. As bhunao (patient frying) evaporates the water, the emulsion eventually breaks and the fat runs free, which is the visible moment three things coincide: the water content has dropped, the sulfurous raw notes of onion and garlic have cooked off, and the spices' aroma compounds have bloomed into the fat. The same physics explains why a well-reduced base keeps for days in the fridge and months frozen: low water activity plus a surface layer of oil gives microbes little to work with. The signal transfers to any slow-fried paste, from Thai curry pastes to Italian soffritto cooked dark.

## Source
Bhunao is foundational Mughlai-derived technique, codified in professional Indian texts such as Krishna Arora's *Theory of Cookery* and transmitted as the kitchen rule "cook till the oil separates." Surfaced while mining the Green Spice Diaries gravy-base videos.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is the flagship case of [[Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer]], and it works because of [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]]: the freed oil is not waste, it is the carrier that has just finished loading the spice aroma.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Reading this signal is what makes a make-ahead gravy base safe and worth building, so it quietly underwrites [[Build, Brighten, Balance]] and the whole [[Cook Once, Eat Twice]] pattern applied to curries.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Tempering]], the other moment Indian cooking hands flavour duty to hot fat, and beside [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]], which is the same cook-the-water-out logic pointed at a surface instead of a sauce.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Health-minded cooks often use too little oil for the separation to ever show, which removes the signal without removing the need for it; the fix is judging by the paste darkening and smelling cooked, not by adding oil you did not want. Rushing on high heat also fakes the signal by scorching before the water is gone.
