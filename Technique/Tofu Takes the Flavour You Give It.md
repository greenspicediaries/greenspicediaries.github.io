# Tofu Takes the Flavour You Give It

## Idea
Tofu is not bland food to be endured; it is a blank protein whose taste is a technique decision, and the beany off-note most people object to can literally be boiled away.

## Definition
The "soy" taste is chemistry: lipoxygenase enzymes oxidise soy's fats into volatile aldehydes, mainly hexanal, the green beany smell. Heat inactivates the enzyme and drives the volatiles off, which is why a few minutes' simmer in salted water (a documented Chinese restaurant practice, cousin to Japanese yudofu) leaves tofu cleaner-tasting, lightly seasoned and firmer. From there, three moves make it disappear into food: match the cut size to the dish so it stops reading as "tofu pieces"; simmer it in a seasoned gravy so salt diffuses in while surface water leaves; or crumble and transform it inside a patty or scramble where it contributes protein and body, not identity. Each move hands tofu a flavour, because it will not bring one.

## Source
Blanching tofu is standard Chinese technique (see Food52, "Why Your Tofu Wants a Bubble Bath"); beany-flavour chemistry per lipoxygenase and hexanal research. Mined from the Green Spice Diaries tofu videos.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It answers the protein problem of [[Protein Complementarity]] in practice, and the boil-to-absorb move is a cousin of [[Tenderise, Then Sear]]: a gentle wet stage that sets up whatever finish comes next.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Once tofu is a flavour-carrier rather than a flavour, it slots into any gravy from the [[Build, Brighten, Balance]] system and absorbs whatever [[Umami Without Meat]] supplies, which is how tofu mutter ends up tasting like the paneer dish it imitates.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It lives beside [[Fat-Soluble Flavour]], because the marinades and gravies doing the flavouring work through fat, and beside paneer, the culturally fluent protein tofu is usually standing in for.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
"Absorption" is often oversold: tofu is dense, and most flavour sits at or near the surface, so technique is doing more of the work than soaking. And disguising tofu concedes the critic's premise; good yudofu argues the opposite, that plain tofu eaten plainly is already the dish.
