# Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer

## Idea
Recipe timings are estimates made in someone else's kitchen, so the reliable instruction is always the sensory signal the timing was standing in for.

## Definition
"Fry the onions for eight minutes" is a proxy. The real instruction is "until they are translucent and smell sweet rather than sharp". Every variable that recipe writing cannot see (pan material and width, burner output, the water content of the vegetable, altitude, how much is in the pan) moves the time while leaving the signal intact. Learning the signals is what makes recipes portable: aroma change in a tempering, the sound of frying dropping in pitch as water leaves, a dough that stops tearing when stretched, a sauce that coats the back of a spoon.

## Source
A constant theme in technique-first cooking instruction, from Samin Nosrat's *Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat* to Kenji López-Alt's testing-based writing.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It belongs to the general distinction between a proxy metric and the thing it measures, and in the kitchen it is forced by the very short windows in techniques like [[Tempering]], where five seconds is the whole margin.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Once signals replace timings, recipes become adjustable rather than authoritative, which is what lets one base method cover many ingredients, which is the premise behind treating [[Dough Hydration]] as a dial. It is also the most durable thing a cooking channel can teach, because a signal transfers to dishes the viewer has never seen and a timing does not.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It is enabled by [[Mise en Place]], which frees the attention that reading signals requires, and it sits beside the specific signals that govern [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]] and the [[Maillard Reaction]].

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
Signals have to be learned, and for a beginner they are far less actionable than a number, and "until it smells right" is useless advice to someone who has never smelled it right. Baking also pushes back hard, since several of its outcomes depend on time and temperature in ways no amount of sensory attention can substitute for.
