# Dough Hydration

## Idea
The water-to-flour ratio, expressed as a percentage, is the single number that predicts how a dough will handle and what crumb it will produce — which makes it a dial rather than a recipe detail.

## Definition
Hydration is water weight as a percentage of flour weight: 500g flour and 375g water is 75% hydration. Low hydration doughs around 55-60% are stiff, easy to handle, and bake tight and chewy — roti, pasta, bagels. High hydration doughs at 75-85% are slack and sticky, hard to shape, and bake open and airy with a thin crisp crust — focaccia, ciabatta, good pizza. The ratio also changes method: wetter doughs want folding and time rather than kneading, and they want to be handled wet-handed rather than floured.

## Source
Standard baker's percentage notation; the handling consequences are laid out in Ken Forkish's *Flour Water Salt Yeast* and across the high-hydration focaccia tradition.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It is the inside-the-food counterpart to [[Crisp Is the Absence of Water]] — the same variable, pointed at structure instead of surface — and it exists because gluten needs water to form a network at all.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Once hydration is a number rather than a vibe, one base dough becomes a whole family of breads adjusted by a single variable, which is a very efficient thing for a recipe channel to own. It also forces a confrontation with flour itself, since a high-protein bread flour absorbs considerably more water than a soft all-purpose one and the same percentage behaves differently in each.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It rhymes with every other ratio-based technique where getting the proportion right matters more than following steps, which is also the logic of an [[Emulsion]], and it belongs to the same mindset as [[Cooking by Signal, Not by Timer]] — you read the dough, not the clock.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
High hydration is objectively harder to work with and more failure-prone, so the "wetter is better" instinct can produce a flat, sticky mess rather than an open crumb. Hydration also cannot be read in isolation — fermentation time, salt, temperature and flour strength all move the outcome, so the number is a strong predictor rather than the whole story.
