# Constraints Create Creativity

## Idea
A narrower set of options produces more inventive work than an open one, because constraint removes the paralysis of infinite choice and forces non-obvious combinations.

## Definition
Unlimited options are not generative; they are a search problem with no gradient. A constraint — five ingredients, no oven, whatever is in the fridge, thirty minutes, one pan — collapses the search space to something a mind can actually explore, and it rules out the obvious first answer, which is usually the only thing that gets in the way of a better one. The constraint also has to be real: a self-imposed limit that can be abandoned mid-task does none of this work.

## Source
A long-standing observation in design and the arts — from Stravinsky on limitation in *Poetics of Music* to the Oulipo writers — and well documented in creativity research on bounded problem-solving.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It belongs to the general finding that bounded problems are easier to solve well than unbounded ones, and in the kitchen it is what makes a fixed [[Pantry as Infrastructure]] generative rather than limiting.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
It explains why the most reliable recipe formats are all constraint formats — five ingredients, three ingredients, one pan, whatever is left — and why [[Leftover Makeover]] produces better dishes than a blank-slate cook. It is also the mechanism underneath [[The Reframe Is the Hook]], where "no sugar" is a constraint that makes both the cooking and the content more interesting.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Consistency Over Intensity]], which is a constraint on effort rather than on materials, and beside the technique constraints that make [[Borrowed Technique, Native Flavour]] work.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Constraints that are too tight stop producing invention and start producing bad work, and the ones that help are usually chosen rather than inherited — poverty of options is not the same thing as a well-set limit. Some problems also genuinely need more resources rather than better framing.
