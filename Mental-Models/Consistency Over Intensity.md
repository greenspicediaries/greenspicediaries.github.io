# Consistency Over Intensity

## Idea
A modest practice sustained for a long time outperforms an intense one that cannot be maintained, because compounding depends on duration rather than on peak effort.

## Definition
Intensity is an easy way to feel like progress is happening and a hard way to make it happen. Any practice whose returns compound — cooking skill, a body, an audience, a garden of notes — is governed by how many periods it runs for, not by how hard any single period went. The practical implication is that the ceiling on effort should be set by what survives a bad week, which usually means choosing a deliberately smaller commitment than you are currently capable of.

## Source
A core idea in habit and training literature; articulated in James Clear's *Atomic Habits* and in Cal Newport's *Slow Productivity*.

## Compass

**Roots** — *where this comes from*
It belongs to the mathematics of compounding, where the exponent is time, and it is the reasoning that makes fixed, unglamorous rituals like [[The Sunday Reset]] and [[Wind-Down as a Boundary]] worth more than occasional heroic effort.

**Paths** — *where this leads*
Applied to a channel it sets the cadence by what is sustainable rather than what is ambitious, which is exactly the problem [[Film Once, Publish Twice]] exists to solve. Applied to cooking it argues for a repeatable weeknight repertoire over an occasional ambitious project, and applied to this garden it argues for one note at a time.

**Neighbors** — *what lives nearby*
It sits beside [[Toxic Productivity]], which is what happens when intensity is mistaken for virtue, and beside [[Constraints Create Creativity]] — both are arguments that a smaller box produces better results than an unbounded one.

**Clash** — *what pushes against this*
Some things genuinely require intensity: a deadline, a launch, learning a hard technique, a week of filming. Consistency can also become a way of never pushing hard enough to improve, where a comfortable daily practice plateaus and the honest answer is that a harder block was needed.
