# Toxic Productivity

## Idea
When the feeling of being productive becomes the goal, effort gets redirected toward whatever is most measurable rather than whatever matters, and rest starts to feel like failure.

## Definition
Toxic productivity is the point at which optimisation turns on itself: routines become checklists to fail at, rest has to be justified as recovery for more work, and activities chosen for enjoyment get instrumented into habits with streaks. The tell is that the metric has detached from the outcome: a four-hour meal prep that was supposed to save time, a wind-down routine performed anxiously, a hobby that now has a tracker. The underlying error is treating productivity as an identity rather than a means.

## Source
Discussed in critiques of hustle culture, including Cal Newport's *Slow Productivity* and Oliver Burkeman's *Four Thousand Weeks*.

## Compass

**Roots:** *where this comes from*
It is the failure mode of every optimisation idea in this garden, and it comes from the easy substitution of a measurable proxy for an unmeasurable goal.

**Paths:** *where this leads*
Naming it is what keeps rituals honest: [[The Sunday Reset]] is supposed to remove decisions, not add an obligation, and [[Wind-Down as a Boundary]] stops working the moment it becomes something to perform. It also sets a limit on how far optimisation should be pushed in content, since a routine filmed for an audience is not automatically a routine that restores anyone.

**Neighbors:** *what lives nearby*
It is the shadow of [[Consistency Over Intensity]], which it inverts by making the streak the point, and it sits beside the licensing logic of [[Healthy Is Not a Permission Slip]], since both are cases where a virtuous frame produces the opposite of its intent.

**Clash:** *what pushes against this*
The label is also an easy excuse, and "that's toxic productivity" can be used to avoid work that genuinely needed doing. Structure, discipline and uncomfortable effort really do produce results, so the critique is of a specific inversion rather than of effort itself.
